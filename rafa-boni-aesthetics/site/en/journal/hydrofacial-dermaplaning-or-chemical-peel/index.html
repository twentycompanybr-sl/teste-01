Hydrofacial, dermaplaning or chemical peel: which facial is right for you?

Three popular facial treatments, explained simply — how they work, who they suit and what to expect afterwards.

Hydrofacial, dermaplaning and chemical peels are three of the most popular facial treatments — and three of the most confused. All of them help the skin look fresher, but they work in different ways and suit different needs. Here is a simple comparison.

Hydrofacial: cleanse, extract, hydrate

A hydrofacial combines deep cleansing, gentle exfoliation, extraction and intense hydration in one treatment, with no needles.

  • Often chosen for: congested pores, dullness and dehydrated skin.
  • Downtime: usually none — many people go straight back to their day.
  • Good to know: it is gentle enough for most skin types and a popular choice before an event.

Dermaplaning: smooth and bright

Dermaplaning uses a sterile blade to gently remove dead skin cells and fine facial hair (“peach fuzz”).

  • Often chosen for: rough texture, dull skin and make-up that doesn’t sit smoothly.
  • Downtime: usually none, but skin can be more sensitive to the sun afterwards.
  • Good to know: it may not be the best option during an active acne breakout.

Chemical peel: renewal and tone

A professional peel uses carefully selected acids to exfoliate the outer layers of the skin and encourage renewal. Peels range from very light to more intensive.

  • Often chosen for: uneven tone and pigmentation, congestion and texture.
  • Downtime: from none to a few days of light flaking, depending on the peel.
  • Good to know: strict sun protection is essential, and some peels are better suited to the autumn and winter months.

So, which one?

As a very general guide: if your main concern is congestion or dehydration, a hydrofacial is often a good starting point; if it is texture and glow, dermaplaning may suit you; and if it is tone and pigmentation, a peel is frequently considered. In practice, the best results usually come from a plan that combines treatments over time with a good home routine.

Your skin type, history, medication and lifestyle all affect which option is safest and most effective — which is why we never choose a treatment without seeing your skin first. A consultation lets us recommend what is genuinely right for you.
